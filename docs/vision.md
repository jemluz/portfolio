# Vision

## Purpose

**turma.dev** exists to be the centralized platform where developers showcase their professional journey — experience, resume, and portfolio — all in one place.

### Why turma.dev Should Exist

Most developers face a common dilemma: create a custom portfolio website from scratch (time-consuming, maintenance burden) or settle for generic platforms that don't reflect their technical identity.

turma.dev solves this by offering:
- A ready-to-use, customizable profile platform
- No need to build your own site
- A professional, developer-focused presentation
- A community where devs can connect and discover each other

### Problem Space

**The Problem:**
Developers waste time building personal portfolio sites instead of focusing on their craft. Existing solutions (LinkedIn, generic portfolio builders) lack the technical personality and discoverability devs need.

**The Solution:**
turma.dev provides a personalized, professional profile platform designed specifically for developers of all levels — from juniors seeking their first job to seniors showcasing decades of experience.

### Guiding Values

1. **Simplicity** — Easy to set up and use, no friction
2. **Personalization** — Each developer maintains their unique identity
3. **Community** — Connection and discoverability among developers
4. **Transparency** — Open-source, built in public

### What Must Remain Invariant

No matter what technologies change, these core features define turma.dev:

- **Visual timeline** of professional experience
- **Public profile** accessible to anyone
- **Resume PDF generation** for traditional applications
- **Project showcase** highlighting technical work

---

## Core Principles

- **Simplicity over complexity**
  Avoid unnecessary features. What we leave out is as important as what we include.

- **Developer identity first**
  The platform should amplify the developer's work, not overshadow it.

- **Community-driven growth**
  Features and improvements come from real developer needs, not assumptions.

- **Open and transparent**
  Code, decisions, and roadmap are public. Built with the community, for the community.

---

## What This Project Is

- A professional profile platform for developers
- A centralized hub for experience, resume, and portfolio
- An open-source project anyone can contribute to
- A developer community for connection and discoverability
- A living, evolving product shaped by user feedback

---

## What This Project Is Not

- **Not a social network** — Focus is on professional showcasing, not feeds or likes
- **Not a job board** — We help you present yourself, but don't replace job platforms
- **Not a resume builder** — It's more than a resume; it's your complete professional identity
- **Not a walled garden** — Open-source, transparent, and community-driven
- **Not overcomplicated** — We intentionally avoid feature bloat

---

## Success Criteria

turma.dev is successful when:

1. **It becomes the reference for dev portfolios**
  When someone says "portfolio for devs," turma.dev comes to mind.

2. **It becomes a meeting point/reference for developers**
  Developers use turma.dev as a go-to place to connect, showcase, and discover other devs.

3. **Better discoverability than LinkedIn**
  Developers are found by opportunities through their turma.dev presence.

4. **Developers get hired using turma.dev profiles**
  Recruiters and hiring managers recognize and value profiles from the platform.

---

## Long-Term Vision

Inspired by the organic growth of projects like **TabNews**, turma.dev started as a personal solution — a better way to present professional experience.

The vision is simple:

- **Start here:** Solve one developer's problem (the founder's portfolio)
- **Grow organically:** Other developers find value and join
- **Build community:** Contributors shape the platform based on real needs
- **Become essential:** The go-to platform for developer profiles

In 2-3 years, turma.dev should be:

- A recognized name in the developer community
- Used by thousands of developers across all experience levels
- Actively maintained by a healthy open-source community
- Continuously evolving based on real user feedback
